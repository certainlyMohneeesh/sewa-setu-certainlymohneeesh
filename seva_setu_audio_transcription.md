# Audio Transcription: Seva Setu Portal Briefing

**Speaker:** Deputy Commissioner (In charge of Seva Setu)  
**To:** Build for Bharat Fellow  

---

Namaste. I am the Deputy Commissioner in charge of Seva Setu, the IT platform for old age pension. So, you are the one they have sent from Build for Bharat Fellowship? Good. Sit, sit. 

You will have to forgive me. I do not have a file to hand you. The vendor who built the Seva Setu portal, their contract ended, and now nobody picks up the phone. Documentation? I do not think we ever got any. So let me just tell you what's landed on my desk this past week, and whatever else comes to mind.

First, an old man walked two hours from his village just to show me his acknowledgment slip, and where his name should be in Assamese, it shows four question marks. Four. He swears he typed it correctly, and I believe him, because he is not the first. Others tell me their names come out cut in half, though they swear they typed it in full before submitting.

Then there's my help desk, overflowing. People writing in, begging us to cancel their application because they made one small mistake, realized it only after submitting, and then found there is no way to fix it. No way to go back. We delete requests, and then they submit again.

Now, before you look at the machine, you need to understand the law. There is a Right to Public Services Act. We call it RTPS. It promises every citizen a decision within a set number of days. And if we stay silent past that window, if we just sit on it, the application is deemed approved. The law says yes on our behalf, and that pension becomes the person's right. For this scheme, that window is 15 days. For years, the overdue files used to clear themselves overnight, the moment they crossed the line. Then, around Holi, it just stopped. Now, files the Act says should be auto-approved are sitting for months, and the Appellate Officer has started asking me why. I am going to be in trouble over this one.

And, uh, another thing. Every month, the two or three days before the disbursement deadline, the whole site crawls to a halt. My staff just shrug: *"Server slowdown hai, sir, kya karein?"* It recovers, of course, the day after it stops mattering. See what is going on there.

One more, and I do not know what to make of it. Meera, my assistant, she says there is a rumor going around that the moment you apply on the portal, you start getting phone calls. Salesmen, people turning up at your door, trying to sell you things. Now, how on earth would that happen?

And lastly, I keep hearing the new digital form just is not friendly. Some people say they are perfectly eligible, but simply cannot get through it. There are even fellows in the bazaar now who'll fill it out for you, for a fee, because only they have figured out how it works. I would like to dismiss it as teething trouble—people always complain—but, well, who knows? We can always do better. Have a look at that too.

Here, let me give you the rules so you have them:
* The pension is for residents 60 years of age and above on the date they apply.
* One application per person.
* The deadline for the department is 15 days, after which deemed approval kicks in, and an untouched file must actually be shown as approved to the citizen.
* And the application window closes at midnight, India Standard Time.

Come back to me once you have fixed what you can. Do not send me a report. Record me a short video—your face and your screen—and tell me the five things you fixed that matter most to these people, in order, and why. And, just as important, what you saw and chose to leave alone.
